      About the piece

      Lágrima (“teardrop”) is a short Romantic prelude in 3/4. Form: A (bars 1–8, E major) ×2 → B (bars 9–16, E minor) ×2 → back to the start (D.C. al Fine) and end at bar 8 (Fine).

      Left-hand basics

      • Thumb behind the neck, roughly opposite finger 2, relaxed – it balances, it doesn’t squeeze.
      • Fingertips close to the fret (just behind the metal), pressing only as hard as needed for a clean note.
      • Curved fingers that land on their tips, so they don’t touch neighbouring strings (the open B must ring!).
      • Guide fingers: when the same finger stays on the same string across a shift, slide it lightly along the string – it steers your hand (bars 1, 5, 12→13).
      • Half barre: finger 1 lies flat across only the strings needed (e.g. strings 1–4 at fret 9 in bar 5). Use arm weight, not thumb squeeze.
      • Shift early and lightly: release pressure, move, then press. Look at the target fret before you move.

      Right hand · Bar 1

      This bar

      p · i · m · a – who plays what?

      p = pulgar (thumb) · i = índice (index) · m = medio (middle) · a = anular (ring). The little finger isn’t used.

      Home position: p covers the bass strings 4–6, i rests near string 3, m near string 2, a near string 1. In Lágrima the thumb plays every bass note and a usually sings the melody on the 1st string.

      Free stroke vs rest stroke

      Free stroke (tirando): the finger plucks and moves up into the palm, clearing the next string. Use it for arpeggios and chords – most of Lágrima.

      Rest stroke (apoyando): the finger plucks through the string and comes to rest on the next (lower-sounding) string. Fuller, warmer tone – great for melody notes when the next string isn’t needed right away.

      Where in Lágrima? Start with free stroke everywhere, bringing out the melody with a slightly firmer a. When comfortable, try rest strokes on the notes marked R: the descending run in bar 14 (D–C–B, A–G–F♯, alternating i–m) and the off-beat melody notes in bar 11. In bars 1–8 the melody on string 1 is followed straight away by the open B, so keep those as firm free strokes.

      Alternate i–m for single-line melody (bar 14): never pluck twice in a row with the same finger; it keeps the line even and relaxed.

      Balance the voices

      Three layers: melody (loudest), bass (medium), inner notes like the repeated open B (softest). Practise playing the melody alone, then add the bass, then the soft inner notes.
